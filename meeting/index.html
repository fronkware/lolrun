          Just for laughs: a joke way to learn how fast zoo animals travel. It isn't safety advice, and no animals are actually loose.

          How the math works

          Two speeds per animal

          Each animal sprints at top speed for as long as it can manage, then settles into a pace it can keep for hours. A cheetah does 64 mph for about 25 seconds, then walks. A wolf only sprints for 20 seconds but trots at 5 mph all day. Cats win up close; dogs, elephants and ostriches win over distance. During a meeting they chase wherever you are, so you can run away at 6 mph, a 10-minute mile. Every 5 seconds of running carries a 1 in 100 chance of getting hit by a car.

          Wildcards

          Rolled fresh each time you start. 🕊️ Pigeons (25%) if you're within 25 miles of a city of 300,000 people or more; they fly in from downtown at 45 mph. 🦈 A shark (10%) if you're within 7.5 miles of the ocean: 30 minutes to get an Uber, load it and set up a temporary tank, then 30 mph, and the tank lasts 15 minutes. 🐝 Bees (4%) from a hive up to 3 miles away, at 15 mph. 🥥 A coconut (1%) lands on you at minute 5, wherever you are.

          The safety score

          Every arrival before your meeting ends adds danger, weighted by how deadly it is and how much of the meeting it's there for. A lion at minute 5 hurts the score far more than a gorilla at minute 55. 100 means nothing arrives in time.

          What this version leaves out

          Animals take either a straight line or the major US highways (Natural Earth), whichever is faster. On a road they go 25% faster, and they cut cross-country for up to 10 mi to get on or off. The heat map is a cheaper fudge: straight-line danger whose edges stretch up to 25% further near roads and blend back away from them. Nobody crosses into Alaska or Hawaii, and Hawaii's zoo animals stay on Oahu. Fences, rivers and traffic are ignored.

          The data

          A hand-built starter set of zoos, sanctuaries and wildlife parks, with one entry per species at each place. Collections change often, so treat it as approximate.

          Your starting spot comes from your internet address (via GeoJS) when the page can reach it. It's rough, often off by a few miles, and nothing is saved.
